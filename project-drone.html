GPS-Denied Drone Navigation

Navigate visually when GPS fails.

This research-heavy project focused on visual localization, simulation, and planning for autonomous drones operating where GPS is unavailable or unreliable.

Hook

Localization becomes the product.

The challenge is not just to fly. It is to know where you are, trust the estimate, and turn that estimate into movement.

Problem

Drones operating in dense, obstructed, or contested environments cannot always rely on GPS. The system therefore needs a reliable visual fallback for localization and path planning.

Approach

  • Built a NetVLAD-based visual localization pipeline using preprocessed satellite imagery.
  • Used AirSim and Unreal Engine for evaluation in a controlled simulation loop.
  • Created preprocessing, training, and planning utilities in Python.
  • Measured model quality against meaningful localization metrics rather than visuals alone.

Your role

Research & Development. I worked across simulation setup, data preparation, localization logic, and the evaluation flow that made the results interpretable.

Impact

  • Achieved 99% accuracy and 95% precision in simulation.
  • Established a repeatable workflow for visual localization and planning experiments.
  • Strengthened my ability to turn research concepts into testable engineering systems.

Learning

This project taught me that autonomy is as much about evaluation discipline as model design. A good perception model only matters when the surrounding pipeline makes the estimate useful and trustworthy.
