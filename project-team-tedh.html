Team TEDH · Founded 2023

Two years of building one team — and winning together, again and again.

I founded Team TEDH and grew the same core group for two full years. We competed across hackathons, drone summits, research showcases, and civic partnerships — and kept placing at different places and levels, from campus events to state-level stages.

Hook

Sustained teams beat one-week hackathon groups.

TEDH is how I learned to recruit, retain, and ship with the same people across Water AI, drones, and real-world deployments — not just pitch decks.

Problem

Most student teams dissolve after a single hackathon. Ambitious work — drones, field AI, civic tech — needs the same people showing up for months, not a weekend. I started TEDH because I wanted a team that could carry projects from idea to podium to deployment.

Approach

  • Founded Team TEDH and kept the core group together across two years of builds and competitions.
  • Entered and won at multiple venues: HackSRM, Smart India Hackathon, Drone Fusion, Amaravati Drone Summit, and research day.
  • Presented to AP Drone Corporation and collaborated with the AP Police Department on civic-facing work.
  • Shipped cross-disciplinary projects — Water AI, tethered drones, navigation — with shared ownership instead of solo silos.

Your role

Founder & team lead. I recruited and held the team together, set technical direction, divided work across hardware and software, and made sure we arrived at every event with something demonstrable — not just slides.

Impact

  • Multiple wins and recognitions across hackathons, summits, and research showcases at different levels.
  • Two-year continuity that produced real systems — including Water AI field collection and drone platforms in this portfolio.
  • Civic and institutional exposure through AP Police and AP Drone Corporation partnerships.
  • Proof that I build teams as deliberately as I build products.

Learning

Winning once is luck; winning repeatedly with the same people is culture. TEDH taught me that morale, clarity, and follow-through matter as much as code — especially when the next competition is only weeks away and the hardware still needs to work on stage.
